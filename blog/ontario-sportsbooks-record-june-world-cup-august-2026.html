Ontario Sportsbooks Posted a Record $1.03B in June on the 2026 World Cup Wave

August 17, 2026 • 7 min read • By PlayCanadianCasino Team

Ontario's regulated sportsbooks just posted their biggest June on record. The latest figures from iGaming Ontario show bettors placed sports wagers totalling approximately CA$1.03 billion in June 2026 across more than 1.3 million active player accounts — up 35% from the CA$768 million wagered in June 2025, and about 6% higher than May 2026.

It marks the eighth time in the past twelve months that Ontario's sports betting has cleared the CA$1 billion mark in total wagers. The first time was in November 2023, about 19 months after the competitive online gambling market opened in April 2022; that month, sports wagers totalled CA$1.04 billion. Since then, only February and March 2026 fell short of the $1B threshold.

The summer is usually a quiet stretch for sportsbooks. The NFL, NHL, and NBA are all between seasons, and the calendar is dominated by MLB, UFC, MLS, and tennis majors — each capable of carrying handle but none of them driving event-level volume comparable to NFL Sundays or March Madness. This June was different. The expanded 2026 FIFA World Cup, which ran from June 11 through July 19 and featured 48 teams across 104 matches, delivered enough betting action to push sportsbooks across the billion-dollar threshold despite the absence of the major North American leagues.

Key Facts — Ontario Sportsbooks, June 2026

  • Total sports wagers: CA$1.03 billion, up 35% YoY from $768M in June 2025 and up 6% MoM from May 2026.
  • Sportsbook NAGGR: CA$78.6 million (20% of total iGaming NAGGR), down 3% MoM but representing the highest non-casino vertical revenue share.
  • Sportsbook share of total handle: 11% (boosted by the World Cup; Ontario's structural non-event-month average is closer to 8-10%).
  • Active player accounts (total iGaming): 1.319 million, up 5% MoM — the second-highest figure in market history.
  • World Cup context: 48 teams, 104 matches, June 11 to July 19; Toronto hosted six matches and Vancouver seven, including Canada's 6-0 win over Qatar on July 18.
  • Eighth $1B+ sports-betting month in twelve: every month from October 2025 through June 2026 except February and March 2026.

Why June Was Different

The 2026 World Cup was the first to be hosted across three countries — the United States, Mexico, and Canada — and the first to feature 48 teams in the field. From an Ontario sportsbook perspective, the tournament's structure was unusually favourable for in-play and parlay wagering: 104 matches across 31 days, multiple kickoff windows per day, and a knockout round that ran deep into the schedule. Toronto hosted six matches and Vancouver seven, including a lopsided 6-0 Canada win over Qatar on July 18 that drew the highest single-match betting volume of the tournament.

Canada's men's national team also reached the knockout round for the first time in the country's World Cup history — a result that, combined with the home-soil matches in Toronto and Vancouver, generated the kind of casual-bettor engagement that operators typically only see during NFL playoffs or March Madness. Sports wagering alone generated CA$78.6 million in gaming revenue for the month, roughly a fifth of Ontario's entire iGaming revenue, even though that total actually slipped about 3% from May's CA$81.3 million.

iGaming Ontario does not break down sports betting by individual sport or event, so the precise share of June's $1.033B that came from World Cup wagering is not publicly disclosed. Industry estimates from the trade press place the World Cup's share at roughly 30-40% of June's sports betting handle, with the remainder coming from the usual summer-base slate. That estimate is consistent with operator-reported "unprecedented" volume on group-stage parlays and knockout-round in-play markets, and it suggests that without the World Cup, June sports betting handle would have come in closer to $700-800 million — strong by historical standards, but well short of the $1B mark.

Casinos and Poker Tell a Different Story

Despite the growth in the online sports betting segment, it's still just a small slice of Ontario's gambling landscape. Online casino games are the dominant vertical: the segment brought in about CA$8.3 billion in total wagers during June, which equals 88% of total market handle. That casino volume generated about CA$316.8 million in revenue, almost four-fifths of the province's entire iGaming take. Casino's 88% share was down just one percentage point from May's 89% — a remarkably stable structural tilt that has held every month since the market launched.

Worth noting is that casino's June total represented a slight step down from May, which was the biggest casino month on record at roughly CA$8.37 billion. June's casino total was still about 30% above the same month a year earlier, but the MoM dip illustrates a familiar seasonal pattern: World Cup wagering draws some of the casual betting activity that would otherwise have flowed to casino games, particularly sportsbook-casino crossover players who maintain accounts on both verticals. The casino hold rate in June came in at roughly 3.8%, slightly above the Year-4 average of 3.7% but consistent with the maturing-product trend documented in our June 2026 monthly report coverage.

Peer-to-peer poker, the smallest vertical, held its usual 1% share of both handle and revenue. June poker handle came in at $126 million, down 6% MoM, with NAGGR of $5.2 million. The segment continues to face structural pressure from live-dealer casino alternatives, and the absence of shared-liquidity arrangements under AGCO's Standards for Internet Gaming means Ontario's poker sites remain ring-fenced against the international player pools that drive volume in other regulated markets. Poker's year-over-year trajectory remains negative — June 2025 handle was $159 million, so June 2026 is down roughly 21% YoY.

Does the Boost Stick Around?

The bigger question for July and August is whether sports betting can hold above $1 billion without the World Cup's tailwind. The historical pattern suggests it will not. Looking at the past twelve months of data, every $1B+ sports-betting month coincided with either NFL season (September through January), March Madness (March), or the 2026 World Cup (June). February and March 2026 narrowly missed the threshold, and April-May hovered in the $900M-$1B range before the World Cup lifted June decisively above the line.

For July specifically, the World Cup's final (July 19) will still appear in the monthly data, but the bulk of group-stage wagering volume drops away. Industry expectations are that July sports betting handle will slip back into the $700-900 million range before rebounding on NFL kickoff in September. The August base case is similar — pre-NFL, the calendar is dominated by MLB, MLS, and UFC, none of which has historically driven Ontario sportsbooks above the $1B mark on its own.

The World Cup's structural lift, in other words, was an event-driven spike on top of an already-strong baseline. The 35% year-over-year growth in June sports betting handle outpaced the 30% YoY growth in total market handle, which suggests the tournament was incremental on top of organic growth rather than the sole driver. But the headline number — $1.03B in wagers, eighth $1B+ month in twelve — overstates the underlying run rate. A more representative steady-state figure for Ontario sports betting, absent a major tournament, is closer to $700-900 million per month.

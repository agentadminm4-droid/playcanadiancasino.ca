AGCO Fines Great Canadian Entertainment $170,000 for Money Laundering Reporting Failures at Pickering Casino Resort

July 7, 2026 • 5 min read • By PlayCanadianCasino Team

The Alcohol and Gaming Commission of Ontario (AGCO) has ordered Great Canadian Entertainment (GCE) to pay $170,000 after a compliance audit of Pickering Casino Resort uncovered systemic failures to identify and report suspicious transactions linked to potential money laundering, the regulator announced on Tuesday, July 7, 2026.

According to the AGCO's findings, the audit identified a pattern of failures by GCE staff at Pickering Casino Resort to "properly assess and track high-risk patrons" who should have been subject to enhanced scrutiny under Ontario's anti-money-laundering (AML) standards. The regulator said mandatory suspicious transaction reports (STRs) were not filed in many instances where patrons displayed potential money-laundering indicators — a category of reporting that Ontario requires at every gaming site operating under an AGCO licence.

The AGCO's release also flagged a procedural failure: GCE had not implemented risk-based policies and procedures for escalating patron behaviour to the level of internal compliance review when indicators stacked. Anti-money-laundering rules under the federal Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act and Ontario's Gaming Control Act require operators to maintain both a written AML program and a working escalation chain that flags patrons whose play patterns diverge from baseline expectations.

What the AGCO's Compliance Audit Found

The penalty issued on July 7, 2026 follows a multi-month compliance audit of Pickering Casino Resort, one of two Greater Toronto Area casino properties operated by GCE (the other being Casino Ajax). The audit, conducted by the AGCO's Compliance and Operational Investigations division, focused on GCE's adherence to Ontario's AML standards — a regulatory regime that the AGCO tightened materially in 2024-2025 in response to provincial pressure to align with federal FINTRAC reporting expectations.

The AGCO's enforcement notice names three specific failure categories. First, GCE's high-risk patron tracking at Pickering Casino Resort did not consistently identify patrons whose behaviour should have escalated into enhanced due-diligence review. Second, mandatory suspicious transaction reports under the federal regime were not filed where the AGCO's compliance audit indicated they should have been — a reporting gap that affects both GCE's own internal controls and the broader provincial intelligence picture that the AGCO builds from operator STR submissions. Third, GCE's written AML program did not include the kind of escalating-procedures language that Ontario regulators require for high-risk patron mitigation.

The $170,000 administrative penalty is the second AGCO enforcement action against GCE in less than two weeks. On June 29, 2026, the AGCO ordered GCE to pay $120,000 for using unauthorized gaming system software at four Ontario casinos — a separate compliance matter, but one that drew from the same Ontario regulatory footprint. The proximity of the two actions suggests the AGCO is signalling an escalated enforcement posture against the operator, particularly with respect to its land-based casino properties.

How the Fine Compares to Recent AGCO AML Enforcement

The $170,000 penalty sits within the established range for AGCO AML-related enforcement actions against land-based casino operators. In August 2025, the AGCO fined a charity casino at the Canadian National Exhibition (CNE) $199,000 for violations of federal anti-money-laundering and anti-terrorism-finance law. In May 2025, the AGCO fined Delta Bingo & Gaming $72,000 for similar AML reporting failures. The new GCE penalty lands in the middle of that range — large enough to signal an enforcement priority but consistent with what the AGCO has assessed as proportionate for the audit findings at Pickering Casino Resort.

The pattern that the AGCO enforcement record describes is worth noting for Ontario players who track how the regulator handles operator-side compliance failures. The recurring issues across multiple Ontario operators — failure to maintain enhanced-due-diligence escalation policies, under-reporting of suspicious transactions, gaps in patron risk-tier classification — point to a category of compliance work that the AGCO has signalled it intends to escalate going forward. The GCE penalty adds to a 12-month enforcement record where AML standards have featured more prominently than in earlier periods of Ontario iGaming regulation.

For Ontario players, the practical implication of the AGCO action is operational rather than gameplay-facing. Pickering Casino Resort remains open and operating under its existing AGCO registration — the $170,000 penalty is an administrative monetary penalty, not a licence suspension. The AGCO's enforcement authority runs through the Gaming Control Act (Ontario) and the operator's registration agreement with the regulator, both of which allow administrative penalties to be issued in parallel to the operator's continued operation.

About Pickering Casino Resort and Great Canadian Entertainment

Pickering Casino Resort is a Durham Region casino property located in Pickering, Ontario, approximately 40 km east of downtown Toronto. The property includes a gaming floor with slots and table games, a hotel and conference centre, and an on-site entertainment complex. Great Canadian Entertainment (GCE) operates Pickering Casino Resort and Casino Ajax (the other GTA property in GCE's portfolio) under AGCO registration, alongside a portfolio of additional casino and racing properties across Ontario.

Great Canadian Entertainment is part of the broader Great Canadian Gaming Corporation / GCE ownership group, one of Canada's largest land-based casino operators. In Ontario, GCE's footprint includes the Durham Region properties (Pickering and Ajax) and additional properties elsewhere in the province. The AGCO's enforcement actions over the past two weeks — the $120,000 unauthorized-software penalty on June 29 and the $170,000 AML-recovery penalty on July 7 — both involve GCE's Ontario properties.

For context, GCE operates land-based casino properties in Ontario alongside the broader AGCO-regulated Ontario iGaming market (the market segment that playcanadiancasino.ca covers in detail). The land-based casino segment and the online iGaming segment are regulated separately by the AGCO — land-based operators hold a separate category of registration from online operators — and compliance audits on land-based properties run through the AGCO's operational investigations division, which deals with physical-venue compliance. Ontario online casino players do not play at GCE's land-based properties and vice versa; the two segments operate under distinct registration frameworks even where the same parent company is involved.

What the AGCO Enforcement Record Signals Going Forward

The AGCO's 2026 enforcement record has tilted sharply toward AML compliance and operational due-diligence. In the past 12 months, the AGCO has issued monetary penalties against operators for violations including unauthorized software use, AML reporting failures, and inadequate patron risk-tier classification — categories that all map to operational compliance rather than product-compliance or player-conduct. The trajectory is consistent with the AGCO's 2024-2025 tightening of AML standards, which was framed as an alignment with federal FINTRAC reporting expectations.

The GCE penalty, like the other 2026 AGCO enforcement actions, is published as a formal monetary penalty decision on the AGCO's website — the AGCO publishes each monetary penalty it issues under its enforcement transparency practices. Operators do not have an appeal pathway at the AGCO monetary penalty level (a separate reconsideration and appeal structure applies to registration decisions and other registration-level actions), which means the $170,000 obligation becomes final when issued.

For Ontario players tracking regulator activity, the AGCO's enforcement record is the most direct read on where the regulator is focusing its compliance audit bandwidth. The 2026 record — including the GCE actions, the Delta Bingo penalty, the CNE charity casino penalty, and a series of related actions — points to an enforcement environment where AML reporting standards are receiving elevated scrutiny from the AGCO's investigative teams. For land-based casino operators, that signals prioritization of the AML compliance program; for online operators, it signals that the AGCO's online-iGaming compliance framework will likely receive the same scrutiny in upcoming audit cycles.

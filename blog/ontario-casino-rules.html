Ontario's iGaming Rules Explained

May 18, 2025 • 6 min read • By PlayCanadianCasino Team

Ontario's regulated online casino market is one of the most transparent and well-governed in the world. Unlike grey markets where players have little recourse, Ontario's framework gives players real protections — if you know what to look for.

This article explains the regulatory bodies, how licensing works, and most importantly, how to verify a casino is legally operating in Ontario.

Who Regulates Online Casinos in Ontario?

The Key Regulators

AGCO (Alcohol Gaming Commission of Ontario)The provincial regulator that issues operator licences and sets the rules all licensed casinos must follow. The AGCO is the top-level authority — no casino can operate legally without their approval.
iGaming Ontario (iGO)A subsidiary of the AGCO that manages the competitive market. iGO oversees the commercial Registrar and operates the Ontario market framework, collecting regulatory fees and ensuring market integrity.
RRP (Registrar's Standards Panel)A body within the AGCO that handles enforcement, investigations, and discipline for licensed operators. If a casino breaks the rules, the RRP takes action.

In simple terms: AGCO sets the rules, iGO manages the market, and RRP enforces them. Together, they form the regulatory framework that Ontario's legal online casinos operate under.

When Did Ontario Legalize Online Casinos?

Ontario's regulated iGaming market opened on April 4, 2022. Before that, the market was grey — offshore sites operated without provincial oversight, and players had no Ontario-based protections.

After April 4, 2022, only AGCO-licensed operators are permitted to accept Ontario players. Any site operating in Ontario without a licence is operating illegally.

⚠️ Important: Just because a casino has a licence somewhere else (UK, Malta, Gibraltar) doesn't mean it's legal in Ontario. It must hold an AGCO operator registration specifically for the Ontario market.

What Does AGCO Licensing Mean for Players?

When a casino holds an AGCO operator registration, it means:

  • ✅ The operator has passed background checks and financial viability assessments
  • ✅ Games are tested for fairness by approved third-party testing agencies (e.g., eCOGRA, iTech Labs)
  • ✅ Random Number Generators (RNGs) are certified and regularly audited
  • ✅ Player funds are held in segregated accounts separate from operating funds
  • ✅ The casino has a responsible gambling framework and self-exclusion tools
  • ✅ Advertising and promotions comply with AGCO rules — no misleading offers
  • ✅ Player disputes can be escalated to the AGCO's dispute resolution process

How to Verify a Casino Is Licensed in Ontario

Before signing up, verify the casino's licence status. You can:

  1. Check the casino's website — licensed casinos are required to display their AGCO licence number in their footer
  2. Use the AGCO's public register — the AGCO publishes a list of all registered operators at agco.ca
  3. Look for iGO confirmation — iGaming Ontario also lists registered operators on its website
  4. Check for grey market indicators — casinos that only accept CAD but don't mention Ontario or the AGCO may be operating illegally

What About Offshore Casinos?

Some Ontario players still use offshore casinos — sites registered in Curaçao, Malta, or Kahnawake that accept Canadian players but don't hold Ontario licences. These sites operate in a legal grey area for Ontario residents:

  • ⚠️ They are not subject to AGCO rules or oversight
  • ⚠️ Player disputes have no Ontario regulatory recourse
  • ⚠️ Responsible gambling tools may be inadequate or absent
  • ⚠️ Funds held with offshore operators may not be protected if the company fails
  • ⚠️ Some banks may decline transactions to unmarked offshore operators

Ontario's 19+ Requirement Explained

Every legal Ontario online casino must:

  • 🔞 Only accept players who are 19 years of age or older
  • 📍 Only allow play from players physically located in Ontario
  • 🛰️ Use geolocation technology (GPS, IP, Wi-Fi) to verify player location at account creation and during play

This means you cannot create an account from outside Ontario, even if you're an Ontario resident travelling. The geolocation check must confirm you're physically in the province.

The competitive market has been good for players — operators compete on bonuses, game selection, and user experience in ways that grey markets don't.

Bottom Line

Ontario's regulated framework exists to protect players. When you play at an AGCO-licensed casino, you have:

  • Regulatory oversight and enforcement if something goes wrong
  • Certified fair games and audited RNGs
  • Segregated player funds and financial accountability
  • A dispute resolution path through the AGCO

Always verify a casino is licensed before signing up. If a site doesn't mention the AGCO or Ontario in its terms, it's not legal for Ontario players — regardless of what licences it holds elsewhere.

Betsson Completes $74M Rhino Entertainment Acquisition, Re-Enters Ontario iGaming via Casino Days

August 5, 2026 • 5 min read • By PlayCanadianCasino Team

What happened

On Monday, August 3, 2026, Stockholm-listed Betsson AB completed the acquisition of Rhino Entertainment Group's Canadian B2C business for a total of €64.5 million (US$74.3 million). The deal was first announced on March 12, 2026, and gives Betsson direct control of Casino Days — an AGCO-registered operator that has been contracted with iGaming Ontario since 2022.

Rhino Entertainment Group, founded in 2020, also operates Big Boost and Lucky Spins. Outside Ontario, the trio runs on Kahnawake Gaming Commission licences out of Quebec. The acquisition is positioned as the foundation of a broader Canadian expansion strategy that begins in Alberta, where Casino Days went live on July 13 as part of that province's initial wave of 22 licensed gambling platforms.

"The target business currently serves Canadian customers and is well-positioned to expand into additional Canadian provinces as local regulatory frameworks continue to evolve," Betsson said in its March 12 statement.

Re-entry, not first entry

Betsson's new Ontario presence is a re-entry. The company first launched in the province through its Betsafe brand in February 2023. Betsafe's Ontario site went dark on March 31, 2026, with a notice telling players the app would be "temporarily unavailable," and the brand is no longer listed in the iGaming Ontario directory of licensed operators. Acquiring Casino Days gives Betsson a swift re-entry under a brand that has been live in Ontario since February 2023.

Casino Days has run continuously under White Star Digital North Limited as the registered AGCO operator. The transition to Betsson ownership changes the parent but does not require a fresh AGCO registration — commercial agreements with iGaming Ontario transfer with the operator.

Why B2B was part of the deal

The headline price covers B2C assets, but the same transaction also transferred a set of Rhino B2B technology assets to Betsson, including front-end and middleware technology that Rhino had been operating. "This technology will strengthen Betsson's B2B offering and is expected to drive incremental licensing revenue within Betsson's B2B business," the company said.

Betsson is one of the larger B2B aggregators in regulated iGaming, running 20 brands across sports betting, online casino, poker, and bingo. Adding Rhino's tech stack onto that base gives Betsson another product line to license to other operators, including the rest of Rhino's pre-deal B2C roster where licensing arrangements permit.

The Alberta angle

Casino Days became one of Alberta's first regulated iGaming operators on July 13, 2026, the day the Alberta iGaming Corporation opened the province's licensing regime to private operators. Alberta is the second Canadian province to legalise private online gambling after Ontario, which opened in April 2022 under iGaming Ontario.

For Betsson, the dual-jurisdiction setup — Ontario + Alberta under the same Casino Days wrapper — is operationally cleaner than running two brand families in two provinces. Expect the company to use this template elsewhere as additional provinces move toward regulation.

What stays the same for Ontario players

Ontario-licensed Casino Days players will not see an immediate shift at login. iGO contracts run to the operator, not the parent, and AGCO's standards for player fund segregation, responsible gambling tools, and game fairness are unchanged. The AGCO-registered entity remains in place; what changes above the line is ownership.

For context on what being AGCO-registered means for player protections, see our AGCO enforcement walkthrough and the recent BetGuard self-exclusion launch coverage.

What it signals for the rest of 2026

Three patterns are now visible in operator M&A in Ontario:

  • Aggregators consolidating player bases. Bigger groups buying smaller Ontario-licensed operators to fold them into multi-jurisdiction product lines. This deal follows similar 2026 transactions at the supplier level.
  • Cross-border brand transfer is the new normal. Rhino ran Casino Days on a Kahnawake licence across Canada; Betsson now owns the Ontario piece through its iGO contract.
  • Alberta is a launching pad, not the destination. A growing list of multi-province operators see Alberta's July 13 launch as a foothold from which to expand when other provinces regulate.

The next trade-press signals to watch: Betsson re-launching under the Betsafe brand in Ontario (technically possible under the new corporate parent), and any supplier-partnership shifts at Casino Days as the new owner rationalises the platform's content roster.

Ontario Court of Appeal Rules 4-1 to Open Regulated Online Gaming to International Players

May 26, 2026 • 6 min read • By PlayCanadianCasino Team

In a decision that could fundamentally reconfigure Ontario's regulated online gambling landscape, the Ontario Court of Appeal has ruled 4-1 in favour of permitting regulated iGaming operators to accept bets from players located outside Canada. The ruling removes a key legal barrier that has kept Ontario's online poker rooms and sportsbooks isolated from international player pools since the market launched in April 2022.

The case centred on whether Ontario's iGaming framework—which requires operators to be registered with the Alcohol and Gaming Commission of Ontario (AGCO) and operate under the conduct of iGaming Ontario—permitted cross-border play. The majority opinion held that regulated sites offering online gaming and sports betting are lawful when permitting players from outside Canada to participate in the same rooms or pools as Ontario residents.

What the Ruling Means for Ontario Players

For everyday Ontario players, the practical effects could be significant. Online poker rooms run by AGCO-licensed operators will eventually be able to merge their player pools with international sites, meaning Ontario players could find themselves competing against opponents in Europe, Latin America, and other regulated markets. Larger player pools typically translate into bigger tournament prize pools, more diverse game selection, and faster action at all stake levels.

For sports bettors, the opening of international betting markets could eventually lead to more competitive odds as Ontario-facing sportsbooks gain access to global liquidity. The ruling also opens the door for daily fantasy sports (DFS) operators licensed in Ontario to offer contests that pool players from multiple countries.

"The decision maintains firewalls against other Canadian provinces and territories," the court noted, emphasising that players in Quebec, British Columbia, Alberta, and other provinces remain unable to participate in Ontario's re-opened games unless their own jurisdictions pass reciprocal legislation. The court added that it expects safeguards to be "rigorously applied" to prevent non-Ontario Canadians from accessing the newly opened pools.

How We Got Here

Ontario launched its regulated iGaming market on April 4, 2022, becoming the first province in Canada to open a competitive online gambling market to private operators. The Alcohol and Gaming Commission of Ontario (AGCO) established the Registrar's Standards for Internet Gaming to govern the new market, with iGaming Ontario overseeing commercial operations. Since launch, the market has grown explosively—handling over $9.52 billion in wagers in January 2026 alone and generating more than $4 billion in gaming revenue for 2025.

However, the market has operated with a significant structural limitation: Ontario's player pool was confined to residents of the province. Unlike the United States, where states with legalised online poker have periodically discussed liquidity-sharing agreements, Ontario's legal framework was interpreted to prohibit cross-border play entirely. The AGCO's standards did not explicitly address the question, leaving operators in a grey zone that prompted formal clarification from the courts.

The question was referred to the Ontario Court of Appeal more than a year ago, and Tuesday's ruling represents the final word on the matter—at least until legislative changes are enacted at the provincial or federal level.

What Remains Unresolved

While Tuesday's ruling is unambiguous on the core legal question, several operational questions remain open. The government has not yet designated which body will be responsible for selecting and approving international partners. Commercial agreements between Ontario operators and foreign platforms will need to be negotiated and reviewed by the AGCO. The timeline for implementation remains unclear, and iGaming Ontario has not yet issued guidance on when operators might begin offering cross-border play.

There are also unresolved questions about how geolocation verification will work in practice. Regulated Ontario operators will need robust systems to confirm that players are physically located in permitted jurisdictions before allowing them into shared pools—a technical challenge that existing geolocation providers have experience solving in other markets but that will require custom implementation for Ontario's regulatory requirements.

Broader Implications for the Canadian iGaming Landscape

Alberta, which has been developing its own iGaming framework modelled in part on Ontario's approach, has entered discussions about potentially aligning its regulations with the new Ontario ruling. If Alberta were to adopt reciprocal rules, the two provinces could share player pools—a prospect that would create a significantly larger North American regulated poker market. The Supreme Court of Canada has also been drawn into related discussions, with Alberta recently entering proceedings regarding the pooling of Ontario player liquidity.

For offshore operators still serving Canadian players in provinces without regulated markets, Tuesday's ruling reinforces the growing gap between legal, regulated play in Ontario and the grey-market alternatives. Research from the Alcohol and Gaming Commission of Ontario shows that more than 83 percent of Ontario players already use regulated platforms—a figure that could rise further as the international pool opening makes regulated sites more attractive to recreational players seeking bigger games and tournaments.

Responsible Gambling Considerations

The expansion of player pools does raise responsible gambling considerations that the AGCO and iGaming Ontario will need to address. Larger international tables and tournament fields could expose some players to more diverse and potentially more addictive forms of gambling stimulation. The existing player protection tools—deposit limits, time limits, self-exclusion—remain in place under the AGCO's standards, and the addition of international pools will not alter those baseline requirements.

Ontario's new centralised self-exclusion tool, BetGuard, which launched in 2026 and allows players aged 19 and older to opt out of all regulated online gaming platforms in the province simultaneously, will continue to apply regardless of whether a player is participating in domestic or cross-border games. For players considering trying online gambling for the first time in an expanded market, iGaming Ontario's responsible gambling resources provide guidance on setting limits and recognising the signs of problematic play.

Looking Ahead

The practical rollout of cross-border play will depend on how quickly the AGCO and iGaming Ontario can develop the regulatory framework to support it. Industry observers expect the first international partnerships to be announced sometime in late 2026 or early 2027, with poker leading the way given the clear demand for larger player pools. Sports betting cross-border liquidity arrangements are likely to follow, though those negotiations are typically more complex.

For now, Ontario's regulated iGaming market stands at an inflection point. The legal barrier has fallen. The question is how quickly the commercial and regulatory machinery can follow.
